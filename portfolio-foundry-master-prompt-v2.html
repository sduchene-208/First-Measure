PORTFOLIO FOUNDRY / MASTER PROMPT V2

Professional Portfolio Website Builder

A reusable, profession-agnostic workflow for transforming a client's existing professional experience into a polished digital portfolio while maintaining accuracy, scope control, client ownership, and a repeatable production process.

Purpose

I want you to help me create a polished professional portfolio website for a client using the client's supplied résumé/CV and intake information as the primary source material.

The goal is not to turn a résumé into a webpage. The goal is to uncover the client's professional story and build a digital portfolio that demonstrates their expertise, accomplishments, work, and value.

The client may work in any profession. Do not assume the client works in technology.

Core principle: AI should accelerate analysis and production, but it must not replace professional judgment. The human service provider is the final quality-control layer before anything is delivered to the client.

PHASE 1 — Collect and Analyze Client Materials

Use the supplied résumé/CV and completed client intake as the primary sources of truth.

  • Professional identity and current role
  • Industry/profession
  • Years and progression of experience
  • Core expertise and capabilities
  • Relevant tools/technologies when applicable
  • Education and credentials
  • Certifications, licenses, awards, memberships, and training when supported
  • Major accomplishments and measurable outcomes
  • Projects, initiatives, research, publications, campaigns, teaching, clinical work, or other relevant evidence
  • Leadership and domain expertise
  • Problems the client solves
  • Types of organizations, people, customers, students, patients, teams, or stakeholders they serve
  • Client goals, target audience, preferred visual direction, and requested content from the intake
Accuracy rule: Never invent employers, titles, dates, degrees, credentials, clients, projects, metrics, responsibilities, technologies, accomplishments, outcomes, publications, awards, or other facts. Missing useful information is a content gap requiring client confirmation.

PHASE 2 — Identify the Professional Story

Before designing anything, determine the client's professional identity, value proposition, differentiators, strongest evidence, target audience, and the narrative connecting that evidence.

PHASE 4 — Create the Portfolio Direction

Produce a concise internal Portfolio Direction before generating code.

  • Professional positioning
  • Primary audience
  • Core story
  • Primary strengths
  • Evidence
  • Recommended sections
  • Visual direction
  • Content gaps
  • Primary CTA
Do not generate the website yet. Present the Portfolio Direction for human approval before proceeding.

PHASE 5 — Determine the Best Visual Story

Identify the strongest process, project, accomplishment, methodology, body of work, or professional concept that demonstrates how the client creates value. Use it as a visual centerpiece when appropriate; otherwise use featured work, a case study, timeline, data visualization, gallery, credentials, or methodology.

PHASE 6 — Build the Website

After approval, build with semantic HTML5, CSS3, vanilla JavaScript, CSS custom properties, Grid/Flexbox, responsive typography, and media queries. Do not use frameworks unless explicitly approved.

PHASE 7 — Website Structure

Use semantic HTML5 such as <header>, <nav>, <main>, <section>, <article>, and <footer>. Include only sections that serve the client's story.

PHASE 8 — Hero

The hero should quickly answer:

WHO IS THIS PERSON?
WHAT DO THEY DO?
WHAT VALUE DO THEY PROVIDE?

Avoid generic “Welcome to my portfolio” copy. Use a strong, evidence-grounded headline and positioning statement.

PHASE 9 — About

Write a professional first-person narrative based on the supplied materials. Do not simply copy the résumé summary. Explain what the client does, what they care about, what problems they solve, how they work, and what differentiates them.

PHASE 10 — Skills and Capabilities

Do not create a giant keyword wall. Group skills into meaningful capabilities appropriate to the profession. Treat software/tools as supporting evidence unless they genuinely define the profession.

PHASE 11 — Projects / Selected Work

Select the strongest evidence supported by the résumé and intake. For each item, emphasize:

CONTEXT
PROBLEM / OBJECTIVE
CLIENT'S ROLE
WHAT THEY DID
SKILLS / APPROACH
OUTCOME
IMPACT

Prioritize story and evidence over lists of responsibilities.

PHASE 12 — Experience

Present career history in a visually engaging way rather than reproducing résumé formatting. Show organization, position, dates, concise role context, and selected accomplishments.

PHASE 13 — Credentials and Resume

Include relevant education, certifications, licenses, awards, memberships, training, publications, and continuing education when supported. If a résumé PDF is supplied, make it downloadable:

/assets/FirstName-LastName-Resume.pdf

PHASE 14 — Contact

Create a profession-appropriate contact section. Use supplied professional links. Do not create a backend form for a static site; use links such as mailto: when appropriate.

PHASE 15 — Privacy and Confidentiality

Never expose confidential or sensitive professional information. Omit or generalize patient, student, client, proprietary company, financial, internal-system, or case-specific information that is not appropriate for public publication.

PHASE 16 — Visual Design

The visual design must fit the client's profession, audience, personality, and goals. Do not automatically use a dark technology aesthetic. Consider sophisticated technical, authoritative financial, calm healthcare, warm education, restrained legal, expressive creative, precise engineering, premium consulting, or practical trades directions as appropriate. These are starting points, not templates.

PHASE 17 — Responsive and Accessible Design

  • Work properly on desktop, laptop, tablet, and mobile.
  • Use Grid/Flexbox; no floats.
  • Use responsive typography.
  • Prevent horizontal overflow.
  • Test long titles and descriptions.
  • Keep buttons touch-friendly.
  • Use meaningful alt text.
  • Use keyboard-friendly navigation and appropriate ARIA attributes.
  • Respect prefers-reduced-motion: reduce.

PHASE 18 — Animation

Use subtle animation only when it improves the experience. Never allow animation to interfere with readability, accessibility, responsiveness, or professional credibility.

PHASE 19 — Content Editing

Make important content easy to modify with clear HTML comments such as:

<!-- EDIT: Replace with the client's professional summary -->

PHASE 20 — Human Quality Control

The AI-generated site must never be delivered directly to the client without human review.

  • Factual accuracy
  • Professional positioning
  • Content quality and tone
  • Scope compliance
  • Design appropriateness
  • Desktop/tablet/mobile rendering
  • Navigation and links
  • Résumé download
  • Contact information
  • Images/assets
  • Obvious console or JavaScript errors
  • Horizontal overflow and overlapping elements

PHASE 21 — Client Review and Revisions

Essential: 1 revision round. Signature: 2 revision rounds.

Revision definition: One consolidated set of requested changes submitted together. Unlimited piecemeal requests are not included.

PHASE 22 — Build Only vs. Build & Launch

Build Only

The client receives the finished portfolio, source files, and deployment guidance. The client handles hosting.

Build & Launch — +$100

The service provider assists with repository setup, GitHub Pages configuration, deployment, desktop/mobile verification, and final live-site testing.

Initial standard hosting method: GitHub Pages. Do not introduce other hosting providers into the standard workflow unless the business owner explicitly expands the offering.

Ownership

The preferred model is for the client to own their GitHub account and repository. The service provider may assist with setup and deployment but should not retain ownership of the client's portfolio by default.

PHASE 24 — Post-Launch Updates

Do not promise ongoing website management unless separately agreed. Future portfolio updates may be offered separately for new projects, certifications, employment, accomplishments, contact changes, or minor content/design updates.

PHASE 25 — Code Quality

Keep the implementation clean, maintainable, accessible, portable, and dependency-light. Use semantic HTML, CSS custom properties, organized CSS, descriptive classes, and vanilla JavaScript.

PHASE 26 — Iterative Development

  • Preserve what is already working.
  • Change what is requested.
  • Do not rewrite unnecessarily.
  • Explain meaningful design changes.
  • Diagnose underlying causes instead of repeatedly patching symptoms.
  • Re-check desktop and mobile after meaningful changes.

Most Important Principle

Do not build a résumé on a webpage.

Build a professional digital portfolio that uses the résumé as source material and demonstrates the value behind the experience.
